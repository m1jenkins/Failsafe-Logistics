## Platform

web

## Users

- Businesses and individuals have equal access to the same dispatch-review process.
- Requesters need courier transport for urgent, scheduled, sensitive, or specialized jobs that originate in the Austin metro.
- A requester may begin through the public quote form, a phone call, or a text message.

## Product Purpose

Speedy Bat Couriers helps businesses and individuals request courier service, then has dispatch review each request before accepting and configuring a job. Product success means maximizing inbound requests through the form, phone, and text while keeping the public promise accurate and privacy-conscious.

## Positioning

Speedy Bat is an Austin-based courier service operated through a small, agile, company-run fleet. It welcomes requests across job sizes and service families, then makes a job-specific acceptance decision instead of presenting broad interest as guaranteed capacity or availability.

## Operating Context

- Requests may be submitted at any time. Submission is not acceptance, and Speedy Bat does not claim universal 24/7 availability.
- Dispatch reviews capacity, vehicle fit, cargo, safety, timing, route, access, handling, and other job-specific requirements before confirming an accepted scope.
- Routine pickup is limited to the Austin metro. Farther cities are destinations for possible Austin-origin jobs, not claims of staffed locations, positioned fleets, or routine local pickup markets.
- Vehicle, routing, custody, tracking, handling, access, coverage, timing, update method, and receipt evidence are confirmed per accepted job when applicable.

## Capabilities and Constraints

The nine current service families are:

1. Same-Day Courier
2. Hot Shot & Expedited Freight
3. Long-Distance Direct Drive
4. Legal Courier & Document Delivery
5. Manufacturing Line-Down Delivery
6. Airport Recovery / NFO / AOG
7. Scheduled & Recurring Routes
8. Air Hand Carry / On-Board Courier
9. High-Value & Secure-Item Courier

- Jobs across sizes are welcomed, but acceptance remains conditional on capacity, vehicle fit, cargo, safety, timing, route, and any other material job requirement.
- “Any job” and “no job too big or small” are positioning invitations, not literal capacity, fit, acceptance, or availability guarantees.
- Public forms and SMS must minimize collection. Do not request or accept health information, patient names, identification numbers, financial account data, access credentials, or detailed descriptions of valuables through those channels. Start with route, timing, a broad cargo category, approximate size or weight, and contact information; dispatch determines an appropriate next step.
- Do not claim regulated medical capability or certifications without verified evidence, including HIPAA or OSHA certification, UN3373 handling, organ or blood transport, specimen transport, or validated cold-chain capability.
- Do not make blanket claims about insurance limits, security, screening, facility access, custody, tracking, vehicle configuration, or dedicated/direct service. Confirm applicable details for the accepted job.
- Do not guarantee pickup or delivery windows, filing outcomes, service of process, chain-of-custody sufficiency, or evidentiary outcomes.
- Accessibility remains an implementation quality concern; no formal accessibility standard is currently a confirmed product commitment.

## Product Principles

1. Give businesses and individuals the same clear path to dispatch review.
2. Maximize inbound form, phone, and text requests without turning invitations into guarantees.
3. Welcome broadly and accept precisely through job-specific capacity, fit, cargo, safety, timing, and route review.
4. Describe Austin coverage honestly: Austin-metro pickup first, with farther markets framed as Austin-origin destinations.
5. Minimize sensitive data and make only claims supported by verified evidence or an accepted job scope.
